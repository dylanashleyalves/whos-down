Who’s Down Privacy Terms

Child safety standards

Last updated: 10 November 2026

Who’s Down is an app for adults to meet people nearby through activities. We have zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE), including child sexual abuse material (CSAM), grooming, sextortion and any sexual content involving minors. This page explains how we prevent it and what we do when it’s reported.

1. Adults only

  • Everyone on Who’s Down must be 18 or over. People enter their date of birth when they sign up, and our server refuses to create a profile for anyone under 18.
  • Everyone must agree to our terms and community guidelines before using the app.
  • Accounts we find belong to someone under 18 are removed.

2. What’s not allowed

It is forbidden to use Who’s Down to:

  • share, request or link to sexual content involving anyone under 18, including drawn or AI-generated content;
  • contact, groom or arrange to meet a minor;
  • sexualise, exploit or threaten a child in any way, including sextortion;
  • post any sexual content at all. Who’s Down isn’t a dating app.

3. How we keep people safe

  • Posts, ideas and bios are checked automatically for sexual words, phone numbers and social media handles, so people can’t use the app to move conversations somewhere private.
  • Direct messages only unlock between two people who have met at an activity and both chose to hang again.
  • The guidelines ask everyone to meet in public places, and activities can’t be held at someone’s home.
  • Anyone can block another user at any time.

4. Reporting in the app

Anyone can report a profile, activity or idea from inside the app. Reasons include “Under 18” and “Inappropriate content”, and a report can include details. Ideas that several people report are hidden automatically until we’ve looked at them.

You can also email us directly at dylanashleyalves@gmail.com.

5. What we do with reports

We treat every child safety report as urgent. When we find CSAE or CSAM, we:

  • remove the content and ban the accounts involved;
  • keep the evidence needed for an investigation, as the law allows;
  • report it to the Singapore Police Force, and to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) or other relevant authorities where required;
  • cooperate with law enforcement requests.

We comply with all child safety laws that apply to us, including the laws of Singapore.

6. Child safety contact

Our designated contact for child safety and CSAM prevention is Dylan Alves, who runs Who’s Down: dylanashleyalves@gmail.com.

If a child is in immediate danger, call the police first. In Singapore, dial 999.
